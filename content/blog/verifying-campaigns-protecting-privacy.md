Trust is essential to fundraising. When someone chooses to support a campaign, they deserve confidence that the campaign is legitimate. At the same time, the person creating that campaign deserves respect for their privacy.

At Pollacle, we believe these two principles should work together. **A campaign should be able to establish credibility without requiring its creator to expose personal information to the entire internet.**

Whether someone is raising support for a school project, a sports team, a community organization, a nonprofit initiative, or a personal goal, trust and privacy should both matter.

## Why Campaign Verification Matters

Online fundraising creates opportunities for people to support causes they care about. But it also raises reasonable questions.

- Is the campaign connected to a real person or organization?
- Does the campaign accurately describe its purpose?
- Is the person creating the campaign authorized to represent the group, school, or organization they claim to represent?
- Are supporters being given enough information to make an informed decision?

Verification can help address these concerns. Appropriate checks can make it harder for someone to misrepresent a campaign and can help establish confidence in the information presented to supporters.

Verification cannot eliminate every risk or guarantee how funds or rewards will ultimately be used. It is one part of a broader commitment to transparency and responsible platform operations.

## Verification Does Not Have to Mean Public Exposure

Imagine a college student creating a campaign to help fund a project. The student may need to establish their identity or demonstrate their connection to the project, but that does not mean their home address, identification documents, personal phone number, or other sensitive details should appear on a public campaign page.

The same principle applies to someone organizing a community fundraiser or managing a campaign for a school club.

**Private verification and public transparency serve different purposes.**

Private verification helps establish whether a campaign and its creator meet the platform's requirements. Public transparency helps supporters understand what a campaign is for, what it hopes to accomplish, and what they can reasonably expect.

The goal is to share the right information with the right people—not to make every piece of personal information publicly accessible.

## What Supporters Should Be Able to See

Protecting privacy does not mean keeping campaigns completely anonymous or hiding important information from supporters.

A campaign page should provide meaningful information about the campaign itself, such as:

- **Campaign purpose:** A clear explanation of the goal and why support is needed.
- **Intended beneficiaries:** Who or what the campaign is intended to support, where appropriate.
- **Campaign status:** Whether the campaign is active and whether any applicable verification steps have been completed.
- **Relevant disclosures:** Clear explanations of how the platform works, how support is generated, and any important limitations.

For an organization-based campaign, the organization's name may be an important part of establishing credibility. For a personal campaign, a public-facing name or limited profile information may be more appropriate.

The exact information displayed should depend on the campaign type, applicable requirements, and the need to protect individuals from unnecessary exposure.

## How Private Verification Could Work at Pollacle

Pollacle's goal is to build a process that separates the information needed to review a campaign from the information displayed to the public.

A privacy-conscious approach could include the following steps:

**1. A creator submits a campaign.**

The creator provides the information necessary to describe the campaign and establish eligibility.

**2. Required information is reviewed privately.**

Depending on the campaign type and applicable requirements, verification could involve confirming a person's identity, checking organizational information, or establishing that a creator is authorized to represent a group.

**3. The campaign receives an appropriate status.**

The platform can communicate whether the relevant review has been completed, is pending, or requires additional information—without publishing sensitive verification documents.

**4. Supporters see the campaign, not a creator's private records.**

The public-facing page can focus on the campaign's purpose, relevant disclosures, and appropriate verification status.

**5. Access to sensitive information is restricted.**

Personal information should be collected only when necessary, protected with appropriate safeguards, and made accessible only to authorized parties with a legitimate need.

These steps describe the direction Pollacle believes in, not a claim that every verification feature is already implemented. Any final process must be designed around the platform's actual capabilities, legal obligations, and security requirements.

## Protecting Privacy Is an Ongoing Responsibility

Privacy is more than removing a person's name from a webpage. Information can still reveal someone's identity through documents, photographs, contact details, or combinations of seemingly harmless details.

A responsible verification process should consider:

- **Data minimization:** Collect only the information actually needed.
- **Restricted access:** Do not expose identity documents or private contact details on public campaign pages.
- **Secure handling:** Apply appropriate technical and organizational safeguards to sensitive information.
- **Clear explanations:** Tell creators why information is requested and how it will be used.
- **Responsible retention:** Establish appropriate rules for keeping and deleting verification information.
- **Fair review:** Provide a reasonable way to resolve errors or challenge an incorrect verification decision.

Privacy protections must also account for the fact that different campaigns have different requirements. A school organization, registered nonprofit, sports team, and individual creator may need different verification procedures.

## What This Means for Pollacle

Pollacle is being built around the idea that people should have ways to support campaigns without unnecessary barriers.

Our original vision centered on turning participation into support for causes and projects. As we develop the platform, trust, responsible verification, and privacy remain important considerations.

We want campaign creators to have a clear path to establishing credibility without being forced to publish sensitive personal information simply to seek support.

We also want supporters to receive honest information. Verification labels should accurately describe what has been checked, and a verified status should never be presented as a guarantee that a campaign is risk-free.

Building that balance requires careful decisions about verification standards, data security, public disclosures, and the different needs of the communities Pollacle serves.

## Trust Should Not Require Giving Up Privacy

People should not have to choose between demonstrating that a campaign is legitimate and protecting their personal lives.

By separating private verification from public campaign information, platforms can work toward a better balance: greater accountability for campaign creators and fewer unnecessary privacy risks for the people behind them.

At Pollacle, we believe trust and privacy belong together. Our goal is to build a platform where campaign legitimacy can be assessed responsibly, supporters can make informed decisions, and personal information is treated with the care it deserves.

**Because supporting a cause should not require putting your private life on public display.**
